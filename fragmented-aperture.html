What Is a Fragmented Aperture Antenna?

Most antennas are designed by hand: an engineer sketches a shape based on textbook knowledge, simulates it, and tweaks dimensions until performance is acceptable. This works well when the design space is small and well-understood — but it breaks down when you need an antenna to be simultaneously broadband, conformal to a curved surface, low-profile, multi-band, and tightly integrated with platform structure.

A fragmented aperture antenna takes a fundamentally different approach. Instead of starting from a known shape, the antenna is treated as a grid of small conducting cells — each one either present or absent. A genetic algorithm searches this enormous design space, evaluating candidate patterns with a full-wave FDTD electromagnetic simulator. Over many generations, the algorithm evolves a fragmented metallic pattern that achieves the required performance — often producing geometries no human would have drawn.

The result: antennas that meet seemingly impossible specifications. Wider bandwidth than a hand-designed equivalent. Conformal to a curved surface. Multiple functions in a single aperture. And because the pattern is two-dimensional, it can be printed on flex circuit, etched on PCB, or laid up in a composite structure.

Why It Matters

The Conventional Antenna Problem

  • Bandwidth is limited by physical size — narrowband by default
  • Conformal designs sacrifice performance
  • Each frequency band needs its own antenna
  • Hand tuning eats engineering hours
  • Platform integration is an afterthought

The Fragmented Aperture Solution

  • Genetic optimization finds broadband geometries directly
  • Curved and faceted surfaces are first-class constraints
  • Multi-band & multi-function in one aperture
  • Automated optimization replaces manual tweaking
  • Platform integration baked into the optimization objective

Applications

Fragmented aperture technology has been applied across defense, commercial, and research domains. Typical use cases include:

Conformal Aircraft Antennas

Low-profile antennas integrated into airframe skin panels, replacing protruding blade and dipole geometries.

Multi-Function Apertures

A single aperture servicing comms, navigation, and EW functions across overlapping frequency bands.

Wideband Phased Arrays

Element-level optimization for arrays that scan across decade bandwidths.

Vehicle & Ground Platform

Antennas that fit existing structural cutouts without compromising performance.

Satellite & Space

Lightweight, conformal designs for spacecraft surfaces and CubeSat form factors.

Low-Observable Platforms

Designs that integrate with stealth-shaped surfaces while meeting RCS constraints.

How We Design One

Our fragmented aperture engagements follow a structured optimization workflow developed and refined over 25 years and three patents:

Define the Optimization Objective

We translate platform requirements — frequency range, polarization, gain, pattern, RCS — into a weighted fitness function. Constraints (size envelope, conformal surface, integration points) become inviolable boundaries.

Discretize the Aperture

The available antenna real estate is divided into a grid of small conducting cells. Grid resolution depends on the highest frequency of interest — typically λ/20 to λ/40 at the upper band edge.

Evolve with Genetic Algorithms

A population of candidate patterns is evaluated via full-wave FDTD. Crossover and mutation operators produce the next generation. Over hundreds of generations, the population converges on patterns that satisfy the fitness function.

Refine with FDTD

Top-performing candidates are re-simulated at high fidelity in our proprietary FDTD solver, including platform effects, dielectric loading, and feed structure details. Fine-tuning happens at this stage.

Validate & Build

We deliver a manufacturable design — patterning files, feed network specification, integration drawings — along with predicted performance and a measurement plan for prototype validation.
