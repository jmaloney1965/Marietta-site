What Are Blade Antennas?

Blade antennas — sometimes called sword, fin, or shark-fin antennas — are the streamlined, aerodynamic antennas you've seen on the spine of every commercial and military aircraft. The form factor looks simple: a thin, vertical blade mounted on the fuselage. The engineering is anything but.

A blade antenna has to deliver broadband performance (often spanning UHF through L-band in a single radiator), survive aerodynamic loads at altitude, integrate cleanly with the airframe ground plane, and meet stringent EMI/EMC and lightning-strike requirements. Get any one of those wrong and the platform either doesn't communicate properly, fails certification, or comes back from flight test with cracked composite.

We design blade antennas — and the matching networks, feed structures, and platform integration models that go with them — using full-wave electromagnetic simulation backed by 30+ years of antenna engineering experience.

Typical Blade Antenna Specs

Every program has unique requirements, but here's the envelope we typically work within:

Frequency Range UHF (225 MHz) through L-band (2.0 GHz) — single or dual blade
Bandwidth Decade bandwidth in a single radiator achievable with proper feed network
VSWR Typical 2:1 across primary band; 3:1 across full operational range
Polarization Vertical (most common) or slant — pattern shaped by ground plane
Pattern Omnidirectional in azimuth (with airframe shadowing); broadside elevation
Power Handling Up to several hundred watts CW for tactical comms applications
Form Factor Typical envelope: 6"–18" tall × 1"–4" thick at base; mounting flange to platform spec
Environmental MIL-STD-810 vibration, thermal, humidity; lightning protection per platform requirement

These are typical ranges — actual specs are driven by your platform, mission, and regulatory requirements.

Recent Work

Sample engagements from our blade antenna practice (anonymized for confidentiality):

UAV Platform · 2024

Decade-Bandwidth Blade for Tactical UAV

Challenge: Single-blade antenna covering 225 MHz – 2.0 GHz for tactical UAV comms, within a 12-inch height envelope and meeting VSWR < 2.5:1 across the band.

What we did: Full-wave FDTD optimization of the radiating element geometry combined with a multi-section matching network. Simulated installed performance on platform CAD model to predict pattern distortion and coupling to existing antennas. Delivered manufacturable design package.

Outcome: Prototype measured within 0.5 dB of simulated gain across the band; VSWR target met with margin. Customer proceeded to flight test on schedule.

Ground Vehicle · 2023

Multi-Antenna Co-Site Resolution for Tactical Vehicle

Challenge: Three-blade antenna farm on a tactical ground vehicle was failing co-site isolation requirements — operator radios were experiencing in-band interference from adjacent transmitters.

What we did: Modeled the full vehicle in FDTD with all three blade antennas in place. Identified coupling paths and pattern distortion contributions from vehicle structure. Recommended antenna repositioning, ground-plane modifications, and one antenna redesign.

Outcome: Co-site isolation improved by 14 dB at the worst-case frequency. No further operator complaints from field units after redesign deployed.
